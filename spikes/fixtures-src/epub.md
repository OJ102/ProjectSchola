# Chapter One: Tides

<span epub:type="pagebreak" id="page_1" title="1"></span>

Tides are driven by the gravitational pull of the moon and, to a lesser degree, the sun.

## Spring and Neap Tides

When the sun and moon align, their pulls add up and produce spring tides with the largest range.

<span epub:type="pagebreak" id="page_2" title="2"></span>

When they sit at right angles, the pulls partly cancel and the range is smallest; these are neap tides.

# Chapter Two: Currents

<span epub:type="pagebreak" id="page_3" title="3"></span>

Ocean currents move heat from the equator toward the poles.

## Thermohaline Circulation

Cold, salty water sinks near the poles and drives a slow global conveyor belt.

- Density depends on temperature.
- Density also depends on salinity.

<span epub:type="pagebreak" id="page_4" title="4"></span>

The conveyor takes roughly a thousand years to complete one loop.
